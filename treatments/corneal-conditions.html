Corneal Conditions

Overview

The cornea is the clear, dome-shaped “front window” of the eye. It plays a key part in focusing light so that you can see clearly. When the cornea becomes swollen, scarred, or loses its smooth shape, vision can become blurred, distorted, or sensitive to light. The cornea is also rich in nerve endings, so conditions affecting it can cause persistent discomfort or a gritty sensation. Assessment may includes detailed imaging of the cornea, which helps to detect early changes and monitor them over time. Depending on the condition, treatment may include conservative management such as monitoring, glasses, contact lense wear, corneal crosslinking, or corneal operations such as corneal transplantation.

Conditions includes:

  • Fuchs' Endothelial Dystrophy: A slowly progressing condition in which the cells that pump fluid out of the cornea gradually fail, leading to swelling and cloudy vision, often worse in the morning. If you also have a cataract, the Fuchs’ needs careful assessment before cataract surgery so that your treatment plan can be agreed in advance.
  • Keratoconus: A condition in which the normally round cornea slowly thins and bulges into a cone shape, causing blurred and distorted vision.
  • Corneal Scarring: Scarring from previous infections, inflammations or injury.
