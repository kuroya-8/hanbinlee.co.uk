Premium Lenses Guide

A cataract operation involves replacing the cloudy natural lens with an artificial lens implant. Several lens designs are available. Some aim to widen the range of vision or correct astigmatism, and these are often called “premium” lenses. No lens can guarantee freedom from glasses, and each design involves trade-offs. The right choice depends on your eyes, your daily activities, and what matters most to you.

Lens options:

  • Monofocal lenses: This is the standard, most widely used lens and a well-established option. It is usually set to give clear vision at one distance, most often distance, so glasses are needed for reading, computer work and other near activities. Glare and night-time visual disturbances are generally less common than with multifocal designs.
  • Enhanced monofocal lenses: These aim to give good distance vision and some extension into intermediate (“arm’s length”) vision. Compared with multifocal designs, they are generally associated with fewer glare and night-time visual disturbances. Reading glasses are usually still needed.
  • Extended depth of focus (EDOF) lenses: These aim to provide distance and intermediate vision, and many people can read large print comfortably. Glare and haloes can occur, but are generally less likely than with trifocal lenses. Reading glasses may still be needed for small print.
  • Trifocal (multifocal) lenses: These are designed to give distance, intermediate and near vision, and offer the greatest chance of reducing dependence on glasses. They can cause haloes, glare and starbursts, particularly at night, and people usually need time to adapt to them.
  • Toric lenses: These correct astigmatism. The lens power is chosen from measurements of your eye, and toric designs can be combined with the lens types above.

Not every lens is suitable for every eye. Other eye conditions, such as dry eye, corneal disease or retinal problems, can make some designs a poorer choice. In some cases, dry eye may need treatment before cataract surgery can go ahead. Some lens options cost more than others (see our fees page).
