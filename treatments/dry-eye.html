Dry Eye Management

Overview

Dry eye is a common condition that can affect everyday activities such as reading, driving, or watching television. Your eyes rely on a thin, healthy layer of tears to stay comfortable and clear. Dry eye occurs when the eyes do not produce enough tears, or when the tears evaporate too quickly because they lack enough of their natural oils. Dry eye is often a long-term condition. The aim of treatment is usually to control symptoms and protect the surface of the eye, rather than to cure it.

Common Symptoms:

  • A burning, stinging, or scratching sensation, as if there is sand in the eye. Fluctuating vision: blurred vision that may clear briefly after blinking. Watery eyes: this can seem surprising, but watering is a common symptom. The eye produces “reflex tears” in response to underlying dryness.
  • Fluctuating Vision: Intermittent blurry vision that seems to clear up slightly when you blink rapidly.
  • Watery Eyes: Surprisingly, constantly watering eyes are often a primary symptom, as your eye creates emergency "reflex tears" to compensate for the underlying dryness.

How dry eye is assessed and managed

Assessment looks at the quality and quantity of your tears, the health of the eyelids and oil glands, and the surface of the eye.

Dry eye and cataract surgery

Dry eye can affect measurements taken before cataract surgery and the choice of lens, and in some cases it needs treatment before surgery can go ahead. See [Cataract Surgery] and [Lenses Guide].
